Why this page exists

The posting asks for more than six months of audio. It asks for a recording setup and a standard that keep producing high-quality audio after the term ends.

This page is how I'd build that standard. It starts with real work you can hear, then a small desk that gives every file the same loudness and finish, a tracker for the whole catalogue, and the system I already run on every wedding I film.

The system I run on every wedding

I built this assistant workflow for Whitetail Films. It does the slow groundwork of transcribing, logging and finding the moments, so every wedding starts in Final Cut with rough cuts instead of a blank timeline. Every creative call stays mine.

What I do first

about 10 minutes
  1. Build the multicams for the ceremony, vows and speeches in Final Cut, the usual way.
  2. Export each one as an .fcpxmld plus an .mp3 into a whisper folder.
  3. Name the couple and the drive and start the run.

What the assistant does

30 to 60 minutes, on my Mac, nothing uploaded
  1. Checks every file. Matches cameras to recorders and flags silent lavs, dead recorders, wrong clocks and stretches with no picture.
  2. Transcribes locally. Whisper writes timestamped transcripts, then names and speakers are pulled out and misheard words and swearing flagged.
  3. Writes three edit plans from a style guide learned from 24 of my finished films. One opens funny, one emotional, one on something specific to the couple.
  4. Builds the rough cuts as Final Cut XMLs, using my angles, letterbox and markers, with every in and out point checked against the audio.
  5. Cuts the full-length films of the ceremony and speeches in my angle style.
  6. Reports back with names to confirm, swearing, gaps and the exact joins to check by ear.

What I do after

the craft
  1. Watch the three versions and pick a direction, or mix the best of each.
  2. Fix, swap and set levels. The assistant never changes a dB.
  3. Add music, colour and finishing, and confirm every name before titles.
  4. Feed back what didn't feel right. It becomes a rule for the next wedding.
  • It never touches original media or Final Cut libraries.
  • It never uploads anything.
  • It never delivers a finished film.
